Atova

We are a Malagasy startup “Atova”, doing app development for businesses in Madagascar. We want to develop a Mobile app and desktop/web solution for these companies that deal in the construction vehicle and big engine rental business that includes power generator, in Madagascar.

Let’s make a lean canvas for this startup.

Atova focuses on app development for businesses in Madagascar.

  • The proximate goal is to create a mobile and desktop/web solution specifically for companies in the construction vehicle and big engine rental business, including power generators.
  • The first task is to develop a Lean Canvas for this startup.

First, let’s recall the Lean Canvas structure.

It’s a one-page business plan with nine building blocks:

  • Problem,
  • Solution,
  • Key Metrics,
  • Unique Value Proposition,
  • Unfair Advantage,
  • Channels,
  • Customer Segments,
  • Cost Structure, and
  • Revenue Streams.

The customer segments for the rental business were identified as:

  • construction companies,
  • utilities & energy providers,
  • mining & heavy industries,
  • events & hospitality, and
  • strategic partners.

Note: Atova’s customers are these rental companies, not the end-users.

The Customer Segments block should focus on rental businesses in Madagascar.

The Problem block should address the pain points of these rental companies.
From the earlier analysis, rental companies face challenges like

  • manual operations,
  • inefficient resource utilization,
  • payment delays, and
  • maintenance tracking issues.

Also, Madagascar’s infrastructure issues like

  • connectivity problems and
  • payment system fragmentation add complexity.

These points should be covered.

For the Solution, Atova needs a digital platform that addresses these problems.
Features like

  • real-time inventory tracking,
  • automated scheduling,
  • maintenance alerts,
  • integrated payments, and
  • offline functionality are crucial.

Since connectivity is an issue in Madagascar, offline support is essential.

Also, multi-language support (English, French and Malagasy) would be necessary.

Key Metrics should track engagement and business impact. Metrics like

  • active users,
  • inventory utilization rates,
  • booking conversion rates,
  • payment processing time, and
  • customer retention rates are relevant.

These help measure how well the solution is adopted and its effectiveness.

Unique Value Proposition (UVP) must highlight what makes Atova stand out.
Emphasizing hyper-localization for Madagascar, such as

  • handling local payment methods (MVola, Airtel Money),
  • offline functionality, and
  • multi-language support.

Bundling features like

  • fuel tracking and
  • preventive maintenance

could differentiate Atova solutions from generic tools.

Unfair Advantage might include Atova’s deep understanding of the local market, existing relationships with rental companies, or local tech expertise. Since Atova is a Malagasy startup, their local knowledge is a significant edge over foreign competitors.

Channels for reaching customers would include

  • direct sales (given the niche market),
  • partnerships with industry associations,
  • digital marketing, and
  • referrals.

Given the B2B nature, trade shows and local business networks would be effective.

Cost Structure involves

  • development,
  • staffing,
  • infrastructure,
  • marketing, and
  • operational costs.

Local hosting might reduce latency, and hiring bilingual support staff is necessary.

Marketing costs should include both digital and traditional methods suitable for Madagascar.

Revenue Streams could be

  • subscription-based (SaaS model),
  • transaction fees for payments,
  • onboarding fees, and
  • premium support.

Tiered pricing based on fleet size or features can cater to different business sizes.

Let’s also consider risks.

Dependency on local internet infrastructure is a risk, which is mitigated by offline functionality.

Payment system integration with local providers is crucial. Ensuring the app is affordable for small rental companies is important for adoption.

Deeper needs might include establishing Atova as a key tech player in Madagascar, creating a scalable product that can expand to other regions, and building partnerships with payment providers and logistics companies.

Atova might also want to position themselves for future funding by showing traction with key metrics.

Validation steps are critical.

  • Starting with an MVP focused on core features and testing with pilot customers helps gather feedback.
  • Iterating based on real-world use ensures the solution fits local needs.
  • Tracking the right metrics early on will guide development and scaling decisions.
  • Balancing affordability with sustainability is key.
    • While keeping costs low for customers,
      Atova needs to ensure their revenue model covers expenses and allows growth. Highlighting cost savings for rental companies, like reduced administrative overhead or better resource utilization, can justify the subscription fees.

Partnerships with payment gateways and logistics services could enhance the platform’s value. For example, integrating with local fuel suppliers for tracking adds functionality that competitors might not offer. This ties into the UVP of being an all-in-one platform tailored for the Malagasy context.

Finally, the Lean Canvas should present a clear, concise plan that addresses local challenges while offering a scalable solution. Ensuring each block aligns with the realities of doing business in Madagascar will make the startup viable and attractive to both customers and potential investors.

Lean Canvas: Atova

(Tech enabler for construction/engine rental companies)

1. Problem

  • Manual Operations: Rental companies use paper/Excel for bookings → errors & double-booking.
  • Low Resource Utilization: 40%+ idle time for generators/vehicles due to poor scheduling.
  • Payment Delays: Cash-based transactions → revenue leakage (15-30% late payments).
  • Maintenance Blackouts: Unplanned downtime from missed servicing (costing $120+/hour).
  • Madagascar-Specific: Spotty connectivity, fragmented payment methods (MVola, Airtel Money), and French/Malagasy language needs.

2. Customer Segments

  • Primary:
    • Malagasy construction vehicle/generator rental SMEs (5-50 assets).
    • Regional branches of national rental firms (e.g., LOCAMAT).
  • Secondary:
    • Mining/utility contractors outsourcing equipment management.
    • Fuel/logistics partners seeking integrated service ecosystems.

3. Solution

A bilingual (FR/MG) mobile + web platform featuring:
- 📅 Smart Booking Hub: Real-time inventory visibility + automated scheduling.
- 💰 Integrated Payments: Support for mobile money (MVola), bank transfers, and cash tracking.
- ⚙️ Predictive Maintenance: Usage-based alerts + repair history logs.
- 📊 Offline-First Dashboard: Syncs data when connectivity resumes.
- ⛽ Fuel Tracking: Consumption analytics for generators/vehicles.

4. Unique Value Proposition

“Maximize your heavy equipment revenue with Madagascar’s first rental OS – operate smarter offline, get paid faster, and stop downtime.”

5. Revenue Streams

  • Tiered SaaS Subscriptions:
    • Basic: $25/month (booking + payments).
    • Pro: $75/month (+ maintenance + fuel tracking).
    • Enterprise: Custom pricing (API integrations).
  • Transaction Fees: 1.5% on mobile money payments.
  • Onboarding Fees: $150/setup (training + data migration).

6. Channels

  • Direct Sales: Field reps targeting Antananarivo/Toamasina rental hubs.
  • Industry Partnerships: Madagascar Construction Federation (FCM), Jirama (utility).
  • Digital: LinkedIn + local radio ads; demo webinars.
  • Referrals: Discounts for client referrals.

7. Key Metrics

  • 🟢 Active Users: 50+ rental companies (Year 1).
  • ⚙️ Asset Utilization Rate: Target 30% increase for clients.
  • 💸 Payment Processing Time: Reduce from 14→3 days avg.
  • 📱 Feature Adoption: >70% use maintenance module.
  • 🔄 Churn Rate: <8% monthly.

8. Cost Structure

  • Tech: Cloud hosting ($1.2k/month), UX/localization dev ($20k upfront).
  • Personnel: 5 FTEs (sales, support, dev) – $4k/month.
  • Marketing: Trade shows + digital ads – $1k/month.
  • Operations: Offline servers, payment gateway fees (0.8%/txn).

9. Unfair Advantage

  • Hyper-Local DNA: Built by Malagasy for Madagascar (language, payments, infrastructure constraints).
  • Rental Industry Embedding: Pilot partnerships with 3 major Antananarivo rental co’s pre-launch.
  • Regulatory Bridge: Compliance with Madagascar’s Data Privacy Law (No. 2014-038).

Risks & Validation Steps

Risk Mitigation Action
Low digital literacy among users ▶️ On-site training + video tutorials in Malagasy
High client acquisition costs ▶️ Partner with equipment dealers for co-marketing
Payment gateway instability ▶️ Multi-provider integration (MVola + Orange Money)
Revenue sustainability ▶️ Freemium model for micro-rentals (1-2 assets)

Next 90-Day Actions:

  1. Build MVP (booking + payments) + test with 3 pilot clients.
  2. Secure $50k grant from BNI Madagascar (tech innovation fund).
  3. Sign partnership with MVola for discounted transaction fees.

Atova’s core insight: Rental companies lose more to operational inefficiencies than competition. By digitizing workflows with Madagascar’s constraints in mind, Atova turns idle assets into profit centers.
